## 8. Known limitations

- The 64 scenarios are built to test the section-9 rules, including every boundary, so perfect classification scores here show the code matches the written policy. They are not a measure of real-world accuracy; that needs labels from real customer outcomes.
- Scenario labels were written by hand from the section-9 rules by one author; the second-person label check and the 50-draft, two-reviewer rubric pass are still to do.
- Activity, bills, consent and contact history are synthetic. Usage decline was generated from the real churn label, so the churn model's ROC-AUC (0.95) is optimistic; on the real Telco columns alone, expect about 0.84.
- Payment behaviour is borrowed from Taiwanese credit-card clients (D7) and mapped onto US telecom bills; repayment profiles keep the overall late share, not individual-level realism.
- Unconditional disparate-impact ratios for partner and dependents fall below 0.8 because tenure (a permitted criterion) differs between these groups in the real data; within the tenure band all ratios are 0.93-1.0.
- Without an API key the system runs in template mode: emails are the approved templates, so groundedness and safety results describe templates plus the guardrails. Re-run with a key to measure LLM drafts.
- Name detection uses a small spaCy model (en_core_web_sm); unusual names can be missed or over-flagged.
- Offers are hand-written (carrier pages block scraping); amounts are in USD only.
- Delivery is mock-only (.eml files); no real email channel, bounce handling or unsubscribe processing.
- The tone check (check 8) is a transparent word list unless an LLM judge is configured; it can only warn.
